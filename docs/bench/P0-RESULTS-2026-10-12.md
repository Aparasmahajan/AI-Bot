What it means:
- **On battery, whisper small can't keep up with a call**, even on 4 threads. The power plan holds the CPU back about 2.8× compared with the run on mains power.
- On mains power, 2 threads should land near 1.5× real time; this needs one run while plugged in to confirm.
- **Design consequence:** during a Hindi or Hinglish call, MWB records both lanes to disk as Opus and transcribes after the call (the design's existing fallback). On battery, or when the CPU is busy, a 60-minute meeting would take about 25 minutes on mains power, longer on battery. That misses the Phase 3 target of notes within 10 minutes, so the target needs revising for Hindi and Hinglish meetings, or a faster engine has to be found.
- Still to measure: real Hinglish audio (P0-7). Hindi produces more tokens than English, so decoding may be slower, and accuracy on code-switched speech is the real unknown.

Findings:
1. **Both lanes run together on PyAudioWPatch without gaps or overflows.** The backup recorder (soundcard and numpy, 12.9 MB) isn't needed.
2. **The loopback lane delivers nothing while nothing plays**: zero packets in 60 s.
   - MWB's recorder must timestamp packets and fill those pauses with silence from the clock, or the two lanes drift apart.

3. **The loopback is taken after the laptop's audio enhancements.**
   - `--self-test` plays a ±1-step 1 kHz square wave (−90.3 dBFS), and it came back at −82.1 dBFS, about 8 dB louder: the Realtek audio enhancements sit before the loopback tap.
   - MWB records what you hear, which suits transcription; it isn't a bit-exact copy.

**What failed, and what it means for the design:**
1. **Dates must be worked out in code.**
   - gpt-oss-20b turned "by Friday" into 2026-10-14; Friday is the 16th.
   - gpt-oss-120b on medium wrote "Thursday, 14 Oct 2026" in the Hindi-and-English notes. Thursday is the 15th, and no check caught it.
   - Commit IDs, times and counts already come from code, and dates must too: the model returns the phrase ("by Friday"), and MWB turns it into a date with the calendar.
2. **Typography clean-up.**
   - gpt-oss writes the hyphen inside IDs as U+2011, a non-breaking hyphen: "PAY‑412", "AI‑Bot". An ID written that way doesn't match a search or a ticket link.
   - Before using any model text, MWB turns U+2010 and U+2011 into "-" and no-break spaces into spaces.
   - With the clean-up, 120b-low went from 40 to 41 of 42; the revised journal prompt fixed the last failure.
3. **The journal prompt must say exactly what it wants.**
   - The first prompt only said "copy commit IDs exactly", and the models went wrong in four ways:
     - they left the IDs out;
     - they added things that weren't in the events: "attached", "a related bug fix", "to capture ideas";
     - one gave the timepass call's length;
     - 20b cited sources as (E1) instead of [E1].
   - The revised prompt fixed 120b on both reasoning settings: "mention each commit by its exact ID; mention timepass calls only as a count: no app, time or length; add nothing that isn't in the events".
   - 20b still drops the IDs. So code should also check that every commit in the facts table appears with its exact ID, and list the commits under the prose.
4. **Small omissions.**
   - 20b and 120b-medium left the Friday sync out of the English notes.
   - 120b-medium left the Monday demo out of the Hindi-and-English notes.
5. **Hindi and English in Devanagari is no problem for Groq.** All three routes wrote correct English notes from the mixed transcript; both low-reasoning routes scored 7/7.

**Decisions for Rev G:**
- **Default API model:** gpt-oss-120b with reasoning effort **low**. It was as accurate as medium on every task and 0.3–1.3 s faster.
- **gpt-oss-20b:** only for the quick jobs it got right every time: labels, dictation clean-up and short answers.
- **Speed:** the design's estimate of "a paragraph in 1 to 2 s" through the API is beaten. Every task took 0.5–1.2 s in total.
- **P0-6 is complete:** `api_first_words_s = 0.56` is now in the machine file. It is the median of the four quick tasks on 120b-low.
