As built (Rev G). Stage A uses the title words (+0.6 for work words, −0.6 for timepass words, with Indian festivals and "chai" among them), the app (−0.3 for a personal app, +0.15 for a work tool), the action items (+0.2 for two or more, −0.1 for none), and your series history, which decides on its own. The organizer, attendees and Outlook categories come with the journal's collectors in Phase 4.

10.2Stage B · transcript check

The model sees the title, the app, the duration, the number of voice lanes and the per-chunk notes and quotes from §9.3, never the raw audio. It has to answer in JSON, with quotes and timestamps as evidence. MWB keeps only evidence quotes that really appear in the notes. Each 10-minute chunk is described separately, so mixed meetings get split minutes. If the check can't run, the stage A label stands, and the reasons say so.

10.3Stage C · your correction

In the Today page, one click confirms or flips a label. MWB stores the correction, applies it to the whole meeting series (the same title without dates and numbers), and adds your five most recent corrections to the stage B prompt as examples. After about 50 labelled meetings, an optional small classifier (logistic regression on the stage A signals plus a sentence embedding of the title) can replace the hand-set weights. It trains in seconds on the CPU.

How accuracy is proven: in week 1 you confirm or flip each label as it appears, and MWB records agreement; the Today page shows the count. The target is 80 % on the first 20 meetings and 85 % after two weeks.

11Memory and model lifecycle

This section is where "uses the least RAM" becomes rules the code has to follow.

11.1Worker lifecycle

11.2Memory guard

  • Before loading a model, the core checks free physical memory with GlobalMemoryStatusEx. It wants the model size plus a 1 GB margin. Between the model size and that line, an interactive model still loads, with a warning in the log; below the model size it refuses and says how much is free. As built: speech refuses below 1.0 GB and loads at MWB's start only with 2 GB free; meeting pieces wait below 0.8 GB; the local model needs 1.25 GB (2B) or 2.42 GB (4B), and stops the idle speech worker first to make room.
  • If memory is short, it tries in order: stop idle workers, pick the next smaller model in the profile, then defer batch work until memory frees up. Language work rarely needs this, because it goes to the API unless the API can't be reached.
  • One model loads at a time. Two may stay resident together only when there is room for both.
  • Idle timers: the speech worker exits 15 minutes after the last dictation, or when its meeting job ends. The language worker exits 5 minutes after its last job on Lean, 30 minutes on Balanced.
  • When free memory stays under 1 GB for 30 s, idle workers exit at once. The core checks every 10 s.

11.3A modelled workday

On the API route, MWB averages about 0.40 GB over the ten hours, about a quarter of Wispr Flow's constant 1.54 GB, while doing meetings, email and the journal on top of dictation. It never goes above Wispr Flow's line: its peak is 1.17 GB, while you dictate. If the API were down all day, the local fallback would average 0.44 GB and go above the line for 6 of 600 minutes, during the email draft and the journal, with a peak of 2.46 GB.
